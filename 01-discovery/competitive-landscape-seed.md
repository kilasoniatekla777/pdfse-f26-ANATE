# Competitive Landscape Seed

## What interviewees actually use today

| Workaround or alternative (as they named it) | The job it does for them | Logs that mention it | What it fails at, in their words (quote + log number) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Messaging the salon or artist on Instagram | First contact: ask for a time, send reference pictures, ask price | 01, 02 | "The back-and-forth was the awkward part. They answered the timing question but missed the question about removal." (01) |
| Phoning the salon | Get a real answer fast: an opening, the total length, a check of the appointment book | 01, 03, 04, 05 | "The first didn't answer, the second was full, and the third said they could fit me in at one thirty." (03) |
| WhatsApp message to the reception | Reschedule with the usual salon | 05 | "The receptionist said it was booked, but my reminder still showed Friday." (05) |
| Group chat with friends plus messages to the salon | Collect each person's service and coordinate a group booking | 06 | "About half an hour of messages between the salon and our group across two days." (06) |
| Searching nearby salons on Maps | Find who is close and open | 03 | "Search results told me who was open, but that didn't mean anyone had an appointment available." (03) |
| Instagram posts and photos of finished work | Judge whether a salon or artist can do the result they want | 02, 04, 06 | "One salon's account showed beautiful work, but it didn't say which technician made it." (02) |
| Online reviews | Check hygiene, punctuality and how customers are treated | 03, 04, 06 | "Some reviews were just a star rating with no explanation." (04) |
| Recommendation from a friend or neighbor | Decide whom to trust | 02, 04 | "A friend's recommendation helped, but I still checked the artist's work." (02) |
| Staying with the usual salon or technician | Skip comparing and explaining from scratch | 01, 04, 05 | "They offered another technician at a more convenient time, but I kept my usual person." (05) |
| Booking pages or links (used for haircut, massage, facial, lashes, not for nails) | Pick a service and time and get an instant confirmation | 01, 03, 05, 06 | "Choosing an available time was easy, although I still called to ask whether the appointment included consultation time." (03) |
| Own calendar or diary | Keep track of the appointment | 01, 05 | "I also briefly kept both times clear in my diary until they confirmed which one was correct." (05) |
| Cutting the service down to fit | Make the slot work when time is short | 02, 03, 06 | "I also had to simplify the design because the artist I wanted didn't have a long enough opening." (02) |
| Walking in without a booking | Offered as a fallback by a salon; rejected | 03 | "I rejected the walk-in suggestion because I couldn't risk waiting." (03) |

## The strongest incumbent

Contacting the salon directly by message or phone. Every one of the six customers did it (01 to 06), and four of them used a booking page for other beauty services but still arranged their nails by talking to the salon (01, 03, 05, 06). Elena says why: she likes "talking to my nail salon when I have a special request." (05)

## Alternatives nobody mentioned

- **Nail-booking or beauty marketplace apps.** No interviewee named one for nails. Booking links came up only for haircuts, massages, facials and lashes (01, 03, 05, 06).
- **Salon-side calendar syncing across channels.** This is in our original idea (`docs/product-idea.md`), but we only interviewed customers, and none of them raised it. Elena's conflicting reminder (05) is the closest case, and it is one interview.
