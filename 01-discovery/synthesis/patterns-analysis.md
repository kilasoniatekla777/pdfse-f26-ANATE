# Patterns Analysis · first synthesis

**Method:** Interpret supplied quotes conservatively, stop the why chain when the next explanation is unsupported, and separate customer descriptions from provider-side assumptions. Quote IDs point to the [complete affinity map](affinity-map.md).

## P1 · Customers need the complete service and finishing time clarified before a nominal opening is usable.

**Interviews:** [01](../interview-logs/01-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), [02](../interview-logs/02-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), [03](../interview-logs/03-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), [06](../interview-logs/06-nail-customer-tbilisi.md)

1. **Why?** A time offer may omit removal, a custom design or the time needed for everyone in a group; Maya asks whether removal fits, Leila simplifies her design, and Amara discovers staggered starts (01-Q3, 02-Q4, 06-Q3).
2. **Why that matters?** Customers have a service outcome or departure constraint that depends on the full duration: Nina must leave by 14:30 and Amara needs everyone ready for dinner (03-Q5, 06-Q4–5).
3. **Why do providers not resolve this earlier?** Not established by customer quotes; stop rather than infer their operations.

**Root cause, within the reported customer episode:** In these cases, an offered start time did not by itself establish that the complete requested service fit the customer’s usable window.

**Unknown / follow-up:** Whether provider scheduling software or staff processes cause missing duration information is unknown. Ask: “Last time, when did you first learn the total finishing time, and what information changed?”

## P2 · Customers choosing unfamiliar professionals seek evidence about the actual technician or treatment, beyond attractive finished-work photos.

**Interviews:** [02](../interview-logs/02-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), [04](../interview-logs/04-nail-customer-tbilisi.md)

1. **Why?** Leila sees work without knowing who produced it, while Sofia sees finished results without learning the treatment technique (02-Q3, 04-Q3).
2. **Why that matters?** Their concerns are specific to execution: Leila wants the particular nail-art capability; Sofia wants gentle cuticle care and clear tool explanations (02-Q5/9, 04-Q5).
3. **Why do providers not resolve this earlier?** Not established by customer quotes; stop rather than infer their operations.

**Root cause, within the reported customer episode:** These customers could not infer the relevant technician skill or treatment process from general salon images and ratings alone.

**Unknown / follow-up:** The logs do not establish whether an attributed portfolio or hygiene information changes actual trust or booking behavior. Ask about the last piece of evidence that made a real choice acceptable.

## P3 · Booking with a known professional reduces comparison and explanation, so familiar customers prioritize continuity.

**Interviews:** [01](../interview-logs/01-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), [02](../interview-logs/02-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), [04](../interview-logs/04-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), [05](../interview-logs/05-nail-customer-tbilisi.md)

1. **Why?** Maya stays with a salon whose manicures last well; Leila’s repeat brow appointment needs no portfolio comparison; Sofia’s old stylist needs no research (01-Q8, 02-Q6, 04-Q6).
2. **Why that matters?** Elena rejects a more convenient new technician because her regular technician already knows the desired shape (05-Q9).
3. **Why do providers not resolve this earlier?** Not established by customer quotes; stop rather than infer their operations.

**Root cause, within the reported customer episode:** Established knowledge of the professional removes some comparison or explanation work and can outweigh a more convenient slot.

**Unknown / follow-up:** The cost of gaining trust and how many visits establish it are unknown. Ask: “What happened on the visit after which you stopped comparing alternatives?”

## P4 · Booking uncertainty can disrupt other plans for much longer than the active messaging or calling takes.

**Interviews:** [01](../interview-logs/01-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), [02](../interview-logs/02-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), [03](../interview-logs/03-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), [04](../interview-logs/04-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), [05](../interview-logs/05-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), [06](../interview-logs/06-nail-customer-tbilisi.md)

1. **Why?** Maya spends about ten active minutes but waits across a day; Leila compares across two evenings; Amara exchanges roughly thirty minutes of messages over two days (01-Q4, 02-Q4, 06-Q4).
2. **Why that matters?** Until details are settled, customers defer or change other plans: Maya holds off dinner, Elena holds two diary slots, and Amara moves dinner by forty-five minutes (01-Q4, 05-Q4, 06-Q4).
3. **Why do providers not resolve this earlier?** Not established by customer quotes; stop rather than infer their operations.

**Root cause, within the reported customer episode:** An unsettled booking can keep another commitment unresolved beyond the time spent actively arranging it.

**Unknown / follow-up:** No independent timing measurement or causal attribution is available, and Sofia says nothing important was missed (04-Q4). Ask for the last actual message timestamps and the plan that changed, with permission.

## Strongest actionable pattern

**P1: complete service and finishing-time clarity.** Logs 01, 02, 03 and 06 connect duration or service-detail uncertainty to the customer's ability to use an offered appointment, with explicit workarounds such as confirming removal or simplifying the service. P4 spans more interviews, but its causes differ; P1 gives a more specific next question without claiming a provider-side mechanism.
