# Affinity Map · first synthesis

**Updated:** October 17, 2026 (logs 07 and 08 added by GT; earlier text from October 8). Eight user-reported real interviews; all participants confirmed by the user as in Tbilisi, outside the course, and consenting to notes and anonymous quotes. Analysis is an interpretation of the supplied answers, not an independently observed session transcript. The hypothesis timing limitation remains disclosed.

Quotes retain the supplied English wording. For 01–06 the original spoken language was not specified; 07 is GT's translation from Georgian and 08 was spoken in English. The inventory below contains all 74 distinct numbered answers (54 from 01–06, 20 from 07–08); the three selected excerpts in each log repeat words already in those answers and are indexed to the same quote IDs rather than counted twice.

## P1 · Customers need the complete service and finishing time clarified before a nominal opening is usable.

**Distinct interviews:** [01](../interview-logs/01-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), [02](../interview-logs/02-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), [03](../interview-logs/03-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), [06](../interview-logs/06-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), [07](../interview-logs/07-nail-customer-tbilisi.md) (5). Contrast: [08](../interview-logs/08-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), where the finish time came with the confirmation and planning was easy.

- **01-Q3:** “The back-and-forth was the awkward part. They answered the timing question but missed the question about removal. I couldn't tell whether the slot included enough time for everything.”
- **01-Q5:** “I called during lunch. The receptionist added removal and said to allow about an hour and fifteen minutes. Then I put it in my calendar.”
- **02-Q4:** “I spent around forty-five minutes comparing work and messaging people over two evenings. I also had to simplify the design because the artist I wanted didn't have a long enough opening.”
- **02-Q5:** “I asked for the name of the artist and examples of similar work. I booked with someone who gave me a price range and explained what would fit into the appointment.”
- **03-Q5:** “I took the confirmed slot and chose regular polish instead of a longer service. I told them I needed to leave by two thirty before agreeing.”
- **03-Q6:** “I booked a massage the week before through a booking page. Choosing an available time was easy, although I still called to ask whether the appointment included consultation time.”
- **06-Q3:** “The offer didn't include removal, and nail art was extra. Also, they had three spaces that afternoon, but only two people could start at the same time. I had assumed we'd all finish together.”
- **06-Q5:** “I asked for a total price for each person and the expected finishing times. We picked simpler designs, accepted staggered starts, and one friend paid the group deposit. We paid her back.”

- **07-Q5:** “When he confirmed the slot in the morning, he reminded me that fixing a broken nail along with a fresh set would take about two full hours. Knowing I wouldn't get out until after 7:00 PM forced me to cancel my evening workout and push off grocery shopping until Friday morning instead.”
- **08-Q5 (counterexample: duration known at confirmation, so planning was easy):** “When he confirmed the 4:00 PM slot, he let me know a full gel polish with precise filing and shaping for piano playing takes about an hour and a half, putting us at a 5:30 PM finish. Knowing exact timing made planning the rest of my evening effortless, so I booked a casual dinner with a friend for 6:00 PM right down the street.”

## P2 · Customers choosing unfamiliar professionals seek evidence about the actual technician or treatment, beyond attractive finished-work photos.

**Distinct interviews:** [02](../interview-logs/02-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), [04](../interview-logs/04-nail-customer-tbilisi.md) (2).

- **02-Q3:** “One salon's account showed beautiful work, but it didn't say which technician made it. They offered me an appointment without explaining whether that person could do the design.”
- **02-Q8:** “I looked for close-up photos of similar designs, especially on shorter nails like mine. A friend's recommendation helped, but I still checked the artist's work.”
- **02-Q9:** “One artist was outside my budget. I ruled out another salon because they couldn't tell me who would do my nails. I wasn't comfortable paying for detailed art without seeing that person's work.”
- **04-Q3:** “Deciding whether to trust a new place. Most photos showed finished nails, which didn't answer my questions about how they work. Some reviews were just a star rating with no explanation.”
- **04-Q5:** “I asked specific questions on the phone about tools and cuticle treatment. Their answers sounded clear, and my neighbor had been there recently, so I booked a basic service to try them.”
- **04-Q8:** “A recent personal recommendation mattered most. Then I looked for detailed reviews about the staff and how customers were treated.”
- **04-Q9:** “I rejected a cheaper salon after reading several recent reviews about rushed service. Another only offered packages with gel, and I wanted a basic manicure.”

## P3 · Booking with a known professional reduces comparison and explanation, so familiar customers prioritize continuity.

**Distinct interviews:** [01](../interview-logs/01-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), [02](../interview-logs/02-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), [04](../interview-logs/04-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), [05](../interview-logs/05-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), [07](../interview-logs/07-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), [08](../interview-logs/08-nail-customer-tbilisi.md) (6).

- **01-Q8:** “My usual nail salon is near the office, and the manicures last well. I only look elsewhere when their hours don't work for me.”
- **02-Q6:** “My recent brow appointment was straightforward. I booked the same person as last time by text. I didn't need to explain a custom result or compare portfolios.”
- **04-Q6:** “I booked a haircut at my old salon while paying for the previous visit. That took a couple of minutes. I already trusted the stylist, so there wasn't a research stage.”
- **05-Q8:** “I already knew where I wanted to go. I contacted reception because they manage the diary; I don't message my technician about appointments.”

I didn't even bother looking into commercial salons in the area because booking someone new on short notice feels risky when you want to look your best for someone you like.”
- **08-Q6:** “I've been going to him for almost a year because he understands the unique needs of musicians. He files my nails to the exact short length required so they don't click against the keys while playing. I didn't even consider looking for another salon because finding someone who gets that technical detail right every time is rare.”

## P5 · At short notice, customers wait on replies and accept an inconvenient slot rather than risk not getting one.

**Distinct interviews:** [03](../interview-logs/03-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), [07](../interview-logs/07-nail-customer-tbilisi.md) (2). Contrast: [08](../interview-logs/08-nail-customer-tbilisi.md), same technician as 07, two days' notice, easy.

- **03-Q3:** “Finding an actual opening. Search results told me who was open, but that didn't mean anyone had an appointment available. One salon suggested walking in without promising anything.”
- **03-Q9:** “I rejected the walk-in suggestion because I couldn't risk waiting. A salon across town had space, but the travel would use up too much of my afternoon.”
- **07-Q2:** “Since I already knew my sister's friend does nails, I pulled out my phone right there in the kitchen, opened WhatsApp, and shot him a message. I asked if he had any opening the next day after my work shift. He took a while to text back because it was late, but the next morning he sent over his availability. We went back and forth a couple of times before settling on a late-afternoon spot, and I saved it to my phone reminders right away.”
- **07-Q3:** “Yeah, the main headache was that he didn't have any normal evening spots left on Thursday. He offered a spot much later than I wanted—right in the middle of my usual afternoon routine when I usually get chores done. I really wanted them ready for Friday's hangout with the guy and I trust his work, so I had to compromise and take a super inconvenient time.”
- **08-Q3 (contrast):** “Honestly, it was super easy! The process was practically seamless because I reached out two days in advance, giving us plenty of flexibility. The only tiny hiccup was that his 3:00 PM slot was already taken, but he offered 4:00 PM instead, which actually worked out even better for me because it gave me an extra hour of practice time at the piano.”

## Not yet a pattern / context and counterexamples

- **05-Q3 and 05-Q5:** conflicting reminders after rescheduling appear in one interview. Do not infer a common salon-record synchronization problem or its technical cause.
- **03-Q2, 03-Q3 and 03-Q9:** explicit difficulty finding a confirmed same-day opening was a single case until log 07; the two together now form P5. Two of eight is still not evidence that most customers have this problem.
- **06-Q2, 06-Q3 and 06-Q5:** group coordination and a group deposit are one customer's episode; do not treat them as default MVP requirements.
- **01-Q6, 05-Q6 and 06-Q6:** customers also reported easier booking-link experiences. These are counterexamples to a claim that booking is always difficult.
- **08-Q3 and 08-Q7:** a regular customer with two days' notice found booking easy; GT noted a possible bias, as she is close to her technician (see log 08, Mom Test slip). Counterexample to booking always being difficult.
- **07-Q9 and 08-Q9:** comparison bookings were an eye exam on a live online calendar (easy) and a piano tuning across several schedules (hard); not nail evidence.
