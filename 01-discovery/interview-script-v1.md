# Interview Script v1 · short-notice nail booking

**Prediction:** We are wrong if the customer does not raise waiting to learn whether a suitable appointment time is free as a difficulty when describing their latest short-notice nail-booking attempt, before we ask about availability or waiting.

**Status:** usable draft; independent cross-team audit and practice have not occurred. Target: someone with a recent short-notice nail-booking attempt who is not enrolled in this course. Confirm actual city rather than assuming Tbilisi.

## Consent · 1 minute

“We are KIU students learning how arranging nail appointments works. This will take about 20 minutes. Is it okay if my teammate takes notes? We will use a code name in our course repository and may include anonymous exact quotes. You can skip a question or stop at any time.”

Record the answer. Ask separately before any recording; notes permission does not permit recording. If they decline notes or quotes, stop the logged interview or agree a narrower use and do not count it as a qualifying quoted log.

## Warm-up · 2 minutes

1. Tell me about the last time you decided to get your nails done. Where were you, and what else was happening that day?

## Core · 12–15 minutes

2. Walk me through arranging that appointment, from your first step to what happened in the end.
3. At which point, if any, did it become difficult? What happened next?
4. What did that difficulty change about your day? What time did you start and finish arranging it?
5. What did you do to get around it? If you are comfortable, can you show the steps while hiding names and private messages?
6. Tell me about another recent time you arranged a nail appointment. How did that one compare?
7. In those two cases, how much notice did you have, and what made you choose that timing?
8. How did you decide whom to contact last time? What information did you actually use?
9. Was there an option you considered and then dropped? What made you drop it?

Do not mention waiting, availability, IERI, features, or deposits before questions 1–3. Mark the first difficulty they name as unprompted. After that, use neutral follow-ups: “And then?”, “Why was that a problem?”, “How long?”, “What happened the last time?” If they independently mention waiting, reconstruct the sequence and durations without adding a cause for them.

## Close · 2 minutes

- Who else with a recent experience like this could we talk to?
- May we contact you again to clarify our notes or show a later test?
