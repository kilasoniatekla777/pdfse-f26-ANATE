**Profile:** 29, project manager. Gets gel manicures roughly every three weeks. Values predictable timing and usually books around work.

### 1. Tell me about the last time you decided to get your nails done.
“My gel was growing out, and I had a client presentation on Thursday. On Monday evening, I decided I wanted an appointment after work on Wednesday.”

### 2. Walk me through arranging that appointment.
“I messaged my usual salon on Instagram and asked for Wednesday after six. They replied the next morning with two times, but one was during work. I asked about removal and the total appointment length, then confirmed the later slot.”

### 3. Where, if anywhere, did it become difficult?
“The back-and-forth was the awkward part. They answered the timing question but missed the question about removal. I couldn't tell whether the slot included enough time for everything.”

### 4. What did that difficulty cost you in time or disruption?
“I probably spent ten minutes writing messages, but the conversation stretched over a day. I held off arranging dinner because I didn't know when I'd finish.”

### 5. What did you do to get around it?
“I called during lunch. The receptionist added removal and said to allow about an hour and fifteen minutes. Then I put it in my calendar.”

### 6. How did another recent booking experience compare?
“I booked a haircut through another salon's booking page. I chose the service and time, and got a confirmation immediately. I knew the hairdresser already, so choosing someone wasn't an issue.”

### 7. How much notice did you have in each case?
“Two days for the nails, about ten days for the haircut. The haircut was easier partly because I had more flexibility.”

### 8. How did you choose whom to contact?
“My usual nail salon is near the office, and the manicures last well. I only look elsewhere when their hours don't work for me.”

### 9. Did you reject any options? Why?
“I rejected the daytime appointment because I'd have to move a meeting. Another salon had a later opening, but it was too far away to reach comfortably after work.”

**Profile:** 24, freelance illustrator. Books occasionally for specific nail art. Prioritizes a technician's portfolio over salon branding.

### 1. Tell me about the last time you decided to get your nails done.
“I was going to a friend's wedding and wanted a floral design to match my outfit. I had reference pictures, but I needed someone who could actually do that style.”

### 2. Walk me through arranging that appointment.
“I saved posts from three nail artists, checked their locations, and messaged two with my reference pictures. I asked whether they could do the design, what it would cost, and whether they had space before the wedding.”

### 3. Where, if anywhere, did it become difficult?
“One salon's account showed beautiful work, but it didn't say which technician made it. They offered me an appointment without explaining whether that person could do the design.”

### 4. What did that difficulty cost you in time or disruption?
“I spent around forty-five minutes comparing work and messaging people over two evenings. I also had to simplify the design because the artist I wanted didn't have a long enough opening.”

### 5. What did you do to get around it?
“I asked for the name of the artist and examples of similar work. I booked with someone who gave me a price range and explained what would fit into the appointment.”

### 6. How did another recent booking experience compare?
“My recent brow appointment was straightforward. I booked the same person as last time by text. I didn't need to explain a custom result or compare portfolios.”

### 7. How much notice did you have in each case?
“About two weeks for the wedding nails and four days for my brows. Even with more notice, the nails took longer to arrange.”

### 8. How did you choose whom to contact?
“I looked for close-up photos of similar designs, especially on shorter nails like mine. A friend's recommendation helped, but I still checked the artist's work.”

### 9. Did you reject any options? Why?
“One artist was outside my budget. I ruled out another salon because they couldn't tell me who would do my nails. I wasn't comfortable paying for detailed art without seeing that person's work.”

### What surprised me

*Reflection supplied with the interview answers:* Having two weeks of notice did not make the booking easy. Confidence in the specific technician and whether the design fit the appointment mattered more than general salon availability.
